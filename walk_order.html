Walk Order Field Guide

WALK ORDER is the roster sort that decides which dog sits at the top of your list, and the next one, all the way down. It's built around your color: dogs you can walk come first, and dogs above your color sink to the bottom, dimmed.

How a dog finds its spot

Three questions, in order

For every dog, WALK ORDER asks the same three questions. A later question only matters when two dogs give the same answer to every earlier one.

1

Which group?

Can you walk the dog? How much activity has it had today? How urgent are its checkboxes? Every dog in a higher group sits above every dog in a lower one.

2

Which color?

Inside a group, hardest dog color first. The toughest dog you can walk leads, so it gets walked while experienced walkers are on shift.

3

Which tiebreaker?

Among dogs in the same group and color: first the dog's state, then potty, priority, how long since its last activity, and yesterday's activity.

Step 2 uses this order. You can walk any dog at or below your color.

Two rules that explain most surprises
  • Yellow and amber ride on the next walk to do. While W1 is open, the color sits on W1. Once W1 is checked, it can only appear on W2.

Where the checkbox colors come from

When W1 and W2 turn yellow or amber

The colors come straight from the roster sheet's own coloring rules. The app follows the sheet. If the two ever disagree, the sheet is right. W1 and W2 follow different rules.

How W1 and W2 differ
  • W1 is about need. Potty, priority and yesterday's activity decide it. Every dog still waiting for its first activity is at least amber.
  • W2 is about time. Hours since W1 decide it. Most W2 checkboxes have no color; amber only shows when a time rule turns it on.
  • Reservations block W1, not W2. A dog reserved for playgroup or training gets no W1 color, but its W2 can still turn yellow or amber.
  • They count different dogs. W1 watches the sheet's "still waiting" count of high-need dogs with no activity. W2 watches how many dogs are still waiting for a first walk, leaving out AR kennels.
W1 yellow

The dog has no activity today, isn't reserved for playgroup or training, and didn't have surgery yesterday. Then any one of these:

  • No activity recorded for it yesterday
  • Bold potty dog
  • Priority 1
  • Priority 2, once the "still waiting" count drops below 5
  • Regular potty dog, once the "still waiting" count drops below 5

The "still waiting" count is the sheet's own tally of high-need dogs with no activity yet. It mostly counts dogs from the list above.

Deprioritized dogs (grey behind the name) only turn yellow when they had no activity yesterday or they're bold potty.

W1 amber

Every dog with no activity yet that isn't reserved for playgroup or training and didn't have surgery yesterday, but doesn't meet a yellow condition.

A dog only gets a W1 color once its kennel and breed are on the sheet.

W2 yellow

W1 is checked and W2 isn't. No more than 15 dogs are still waiting for their first walk. Then either:

  • Bold potty dog, 5+ hours since its first walk
  • Priority above 0, 6+ hours since its first walk
W2 amber

W1 is checked and W2 isn't, and either:

  • Any potty dog, 7+ hours since its first walk, with 10 or fewer dogs still waiting for a first walk
  • First walk was before 10 AM, with fewer than 10 dogs still waiting for a first walk

Which tiebreakers actually matter where

Some tiebreakers can't come up in some groups, because the same thing that would trigger them also turns W1 yellow.

  • Yellow groups (1, 9): every tiebreaker is live. These are exactly the dogs that are bold potty, had no activity yesterday, or are priority 1, so the tiebreakers sort them by which reason applies.
  • Amber groups (2, 10): the top four tiebreakers are effectively never in play, since a dog that matched any of them would be yellow. In practice amber dogs order by regular potty → priority number → quieter yesterday, then the shuffle.
  • Not-urgent groups (3, 11): reserved and post-surgery dogs. The state order usually decides it before the tiebreakers are needed.

Example: a Gold walker's amber group, mid-morning. Six dogs, no activity yet, all W1 amber.

Mango and Biscuit lead because Gold is the hardest color here. Between them, Mango is a regular potty dog and Biscuit isn't.

Pepper, Tank and Ranger are all Blue. None is a potty dog, and Pepper and Tank have no priority set, which puts them above Ranger's −1. Between Pepper and Tank, Pepper had the quieter yesterday: 1 walk against Tank's playgroup.

Juno (Aqua) comes last. It's the easiest color in this group, so no tiebreaker can lift it above the Blue dogs.

Common questions

Why did a dog jump up or down while I was looking?

The list re-checks every minute and whenever anyone logs a walk or playgroup. W2 colors depend on the clock (hours since W1) and on how many dogs are still waiting, so they turn on by themselves. Another walker logging a walk anywhere on the roster can also shift the "still waiting" counts and turn checkboxes yellow or amber on dogs nobody touched.

Why does my list look different from another walker's?

The list is built around your color. A Blue walker's top dog may be a Gold walker's sixth, because the Gold walker's list starts with Gold dogs. Dogs above your color also differ: what's dimmed for one walker is fully walkable for another.

Why isn't the same dog first every morning?

The daily shuffle. When dogs are genuinely equal on everything (same group, color, state, potty, priority and yesterday) they'd otherwise sit in sheet order, so the same dog would lead every day. The shuffle re-deals them at midnight. Every phone sees the same deal on the same day, so two walkers comparing lists will agree.

A dog with a yellow W2 is sitting low. Why?

Check its PG checkbox. A dog with W1 checked and a lime playgroup reservation (S10, S11) stays in the not-urgent one-activity group (6) even when its W2 is yellow or amber, because activity is already lined up for it. If the reservation falls through, remove it and the dog moves up to group 4 or 5.

A deprioritized dog suddenly jumped near the top. Is that right?

Yes. Priority below zero normally pushes a dog down, but a dog with no activity yesterday still turns yellow, and among dogs with no activity yet it ranks right after priority 1. Low priority is meant to lower a dog in the everyday order, not to leave it in its kennel two days running.

A surgery dog isn't showing yellow or amber at all.

Expected. A dog with "surgery yesterday" never gets a W1 color. It sits in the not-urgent group (3), and its quiet-yesterday score puts it at the end of any tie. Its dog page shows the "Recent surgery - NO RUNNING" flag.

Why is a Blue- dog above a Blue dog?

You're on WALK ORDER 2, which bundles Blue- with Blue (and Gold- with Gold) for dogs with no activity yet. See bundling. WALK ORDER always puts Blue above Blue-.

The order on my phone doesn't match this guide.

First check the sort menu says WALK ORDER, not WALK ORDER 2. Right after an app update, a phone that already had the app open can keep running the old version. Fully close and reopen the app. If it still doesn't match, note the dog and the time and pass it along.
