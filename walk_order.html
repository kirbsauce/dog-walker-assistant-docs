Walk Order Field Guide

WALK ORDER is the roster sort that decides which dog sits at the top of a walker's list, and the next one, all the way down. This guide explains every rule in the words walkers use at the kennel: cell colors, grades, potty dogs, priority and yesterday's activity.

The short version
  1. The list is personal. It's built around the grade of whoever is looking at it. Dogs you can walk come first; dogs above your grade sink to the bottom, faded.
  2. Fewest outings first. A dog that hasn't been out today always sits above one that has. A dog with one outing sits above one with two.
  3. Bright yellow, then amber, then not urgent. Within each outing count, cell urgency decides which section a dog lands in.
  4. Inside a section, hardest grade first. The toughest dog you're cleared for leads, so it gets walked while experienced walkers are on shift.
  5. Grade pairs before the first outing. For dogs that haven't been out yet, Blue- counts the same as Blue, and Gold- counts the same as Gold. Potty, priority and yesterday's activity decide the order within the pair, so a minus dog that needs out more goes first.

How a dog finds its spot

Three questions, in order

For every dog, WALK ORDER asks the same three questions. The first one decides the section. The second and third decide the spot inside it. A later question only matters when two dogs give the same answer to every earlier one.

1

Which section?

Can this walker walk the dog? How many outings has it had today? How urgent are its cells? The sections are listed in the next part of this guide.

2

Which grade?

Hardest grade first. For dogs that haven't been out, Blue- and Blue tie, and Gold- and Gold tie. For dogs that have been out, every grade stands on its own.

3

Which tie-breaker?

First the dog's exact cell combination, then potty, priority, how long since its last outing, and how busy yesterday was. The full ladders are further down.

Every dog on the roster lands in exactly one of these sections. A section with no dogs in it simply doesn't show. Sections never interleave: every dog in section 3 sits above every dog in section 4.

Inside a section

The tie-breaker ladders

Once two dogs share a section, a grade (or a grade pair), and a cell combination, WALK ORDER walks down a ladder. The first rung where they differ decides it. Dogs that haven't been out use a different ladder from dogs that have.

Which rungs actually matter where

Some rungs can't come up in some sections, because the same thing that would trigger them also turns the cell bright yellow.

  • Bright yellow sections (1, 9): every rung is live. These are exactly the dogs that are bold potty, have nothing from yesterday, or are priority 1, so the ladder sorts them by which reason applies.
  • Amber sections (2, 10): the top four rungs are effectively never in play, since a dog that matched any of them would be bright yellow. In practice amber dogs order by regular potty → priority number → quieter yesterday, then true grade within a pair, then the shuffle.
  • Nothing-urgent sections (3, 11): reserved and post-surgery dogs. The cell combination order shown above usually decides it before the ladder is needed.
Grade pairs in action

How Blue- and Gold- dogs are placed

Until a dog's first outing, a minus grade counts the same as the full grade. A Blue- dog that hasn't been out isn't stuck below every Blue dog in its section. If it needs out more, it goes first. After the first outing, the minus counts again and Blue sits above Blue-.

Example: a Gold walker's amber section, mid-morning. Six dogs, none out yet, all W1 amber.

Mango (Gold-, purple name) goes ahead of Biscuit (Gold). They count as the same grade, and Mango is a regular potty dog while Biscuit isn't.

Pepper (Blue-) goes ahead of Tank (Blue) and Ranger (Blue). None of the three is a potty dog, and Pepper and Tank have no priority set, which puts them above Ranger's −1. Between Pepper and Tank, Pepper had the quieter yesterday: 1 walk against Tank's playgroup.

Juno (Aqua) comes last. Aqua isn't part of a pair, and it's the easiest grade in this section.

Where the colors come from

When cells turn yellow and amber

The urgency colors come straight from the roster sheet's own coloring rules. The app follows the sheet. If the two ever disagree, the sheet is right. Here is what turns each cell on.

W1 bright yellow

The dog hasn't been walked or to playgroup today, isn't booked for playgroup or training, and didn't have surgery yesterday. Then any one of these:

  • Nothing recorded for it yesterday
  • Bold potty dog
  • Priority 1
  • Priority 2, once the "still waiting" count drops below 5
  • Regular potty dog, once the "still waiting" count drops below 5

The "still waiting" count is the sheet's own tally of high-need dogs that haven't been out yet. It mostly counts dogs from the list above.

Deprioritized dogs (grey behind the name) only go bright yellow when nothing was recorded for them yesterday or they're bold potty.

W1 amber

Every dog that hasn't been out, isn't booked for playgroup or training, and didn't have surgery yesterday, but doesn't meet a bright yellow condition.

A dog only gets a W1 color once its kennel and breed are on the sheet.

W2 bright yellow

W1 is done and W2 isn't. No more than 15 dogs on the roster are still waiting for their first walk. Then either:

  • Bold potty dog, 5+ hours since its first walk
  • Priority above 0, 6+ hours since its first walk
W2 amber

W1 is done and W2 isn't, and either:

  • Any potty dog, 7+ hours since its first walk, with 10 or fewer dogs still waiting for a first walk
  • First walk was before 10 AM, with fewer than 10 dogs still waiting for a first walk

The W2 count of dogs waiting for a first walk leaves out AR kennels.

W1 cyan

Booked for training and W1 not done yet. Once W1 is checked, the reservation counts as used.

PG lime

Booked for playgroup and not attended yet. It's independent of W1: the booking stays until the dog actually goes to playgroup.

Two rules that explain most surprises
  • Yellow and amber ride on the next walk to do. While W1 is open, the color sits on W1. Once W1 is checked, it can only appear on W2.
  • A check wipes the cell's old color. Checking W1 on a cyan dog ends the training booking. A playgroup check ends the PG booking.

FAQ

Common questions

Why is a Blue- dog above a Blue dog?

That's the WALK ORDER rule. Before a dog's first outing, Blue- and Blue are the same grade for ordering purposes. The Blue- dog won on potty, priority, or a quieter yesterday. Once both dogs have been out, Blue goes back above Blue-.

Why did a dog jump up or down while I was looking?

The list re-checks every minute and after every checkbox. W2 colors depend on the clock (hours since W1) and on how many dogs are still waiting, so they turn on by themselves. Another walker checking a box anywhere on the roster can also shift the "still waiting" counts and light up cells on dogs nobody touched.

Why does my list look different from another walker's?

The list is built around each viewer's grade. A Blue walker's top dog may be a Gold walker's sixth, because the Gold walker's list starts with Gold dogs. Above-grade dogs also differ: what's faded for one walker is fully walkable for another.

Why isn't the same dog first every morning?

The daily shuffle. When dogs are genuinely equal on everything (same section, grade, cells, potty, priority and yesterday) they'd otherwise sit in sheet order, so the same dog would lead every day. The shuffle re-deals them at midnight. Every phone sees the same deal on the same day, so two walkers comparing lists will agree.

A dog with a bright yellow W2 is sitting low. Why?

Check its PG cell. A dog with W1 done and a lime playgroup booking stays in the "nothing urgent" one-outing section (6) even when its W2 is lit, because an outing is already lined up for it. If the booking falls through, uncheck the reservation and the dog moves up to section 4.

A deprioritized dog suddenly jumped near the top. Is that right?

Yes. Priority below zero normally pushes a dog down, but a dog with nothing recorded yesterday still goes bright yellow, and among dogs that haven't been out it ranks right after priority 1. Low priority is meant to lower a dog in the everyday order, not to leave it in its kennel two days running.

A surgery dog isn't showing yellow or amber at all.

Expected. A dog with "surgery yesterday" never gets a W1 urgency color. It sits in the "nothing urgent" section (3), and its quiet yesterday score puts it at the end of any tie. Its dog page shows the "Recent surgery - NO RUNNING" flag.

The order on my phone doesn't match this guide.

Right after an app update, a phone that already had the app open can keep running the old version. Fully close and reopen the app. If it still doesn't match, note the dog and the time and pass it along.
