Walk Order Field Guide

WALK ORDER is the roster sort that decides which dog sits at the top of your list, and the next one, all the way down. It's built around your color: dogs you can walk come first, and dogs above your color sink to the bottom, dimmed.

How a dog finds its spot

Three questions, in order

For every dog, WALK ORDER asks the same three questions. A later question only matters when two dogs give the same answer to every earlier one.

1

Which group?

Can you walk the dog? How much activity has it had today? How urgent are its checkboxes: high, medium or low? Every dog in an earlier group sits above every dog in a later one.

2

Which color?

Inside a group, hardest dog color first. The toughest dog you can walk leads, so it gets walked while experienced walkers are on shift. Before a dog's first activity, Blue- and Gold- are bundled with Blue and Gold.

3

Final order

Among dogs in the same group, color and state: priority, potty, how long since its last activity, and amount of activity yesterday.

Checkbox colors

Every roster row has three checkboxes: W1, W2 and PG (labelled 1, 2 and P on the roster). Their colors drive everything below. A walk or a playgroup visit counts as activity.

  • YellowHigh urgency.
  • AmberMedium urgency.
  • No colorLow urgency.
  • CyanW1 only. Reserved for training.
  • LimePG only. Reserved for playgroup.
  • Green, checkedPG only. Went to playgroup.
  • Grey, checkedWalk logged.

Step 1 – Which group?

Twelve groups, always in this order

Each group is a set of states, split by whether you can walk the dog. Every dog lands in exactly one group, and every dog in a group sits above every dog in the next. A group with no dogs in it doesn't show.

Blue- / Blue and Gold- / Gold bundling

Before a dog's first activity, Blue- and Blue count as one color, and so do Gold- and Gold. A minus dog that needs out more isn't stuck below every full-color dog.

  • Only before the first activity. In G1 – G3 and G9 – G11, Blue- counts as the same color as Blue, and Gold- the same as Gold. Once a dog has had activity, Blue sits above Blue- again.
  • The state and final-order rules decide inside a bundle. A Blue- bold potty dog goes ahead of a plain Blue dog.
  • True color comes last. If two dogs in a bundle tie on every other rule, Blue goes before Blue-, and Gold before Gold-, before the daily shuffle. The shuffle never mixes them.

Common questions

Why is my list like this?

Why does my list look different from another walker's?

The list is built around your color. A Blue walker's top dog may be a Gold walker's sixth, because the Gold walker's list starts with Gold dogs. Dogs above your color also differ: what's dimmed for one walker is fully walkable for another.

Why are some dogs dimmed?

They're above your color, so you can't walk them. They sit at the bottom of your list (G9 – G12) so you can still see them. A walker with a harder color sees them as regular rows, often near the top.

Why isn't the same dog first every morning?

The daily shuffle. Among dogs with no activity yet that are equal on everything (same group, color, state, potty, priority and activity yesterday), the shuffle re-deals the order at midnight, so the same dog doesn't lead every day. Every phone sees the same deal on the same day, so two walkers comparing lists will agree. Once dogs have had activity, ties keep their roster order.

Why is this dog here?

A dog with a high-urgency W2 is sitting low. Why?

Two common reasons:

  • Dogs still waiting for their first activity come first. Its W2 puts it in G4, below every dog you can walk in G1 – G3, even low-urgency or reserved ones.
  • It's reserved for playgroup. A dog with W1 checked and a lime PG reservation (S10, S11) stays in G6 even when its W2 is high or medium urgency, because activity is already lined up. If the reservation falls through, remove it and the dog moves up to G4 or G5.
A deprioritized dog suddenly jumped near the top. Is that right?

Yes. Being deprioritized normally pushes a dog down, but a dog with no activity yesterday still turns high urgency, and among dogs with no activity yet it ranks right after priority 1. Deprioritizing is meant to lower a dog in the everyday order, not to leave it in its kennel two days running.

Why is a Blue- dog above a Blue dog?

Before a dog's first activity, Blue- and Blue are bundled into one color, so the Blue- dog won on its state, potty, priority or amount of activity yesterday. Once both dogs have had activity, Blue goes back above Blue-.

Why is a dog with "S" in the Y column low urgency?

"S" in the Y column marks a surgery. Leads add it when they suspect a dog has had surgery, to lower its urgency. Its W1 never turns high or medium urgency, so until its first activity it sits in G3, and it goes last in any tie. After its first walk, W2 follows the normal rules.

Why did it change?

Why did a dog jump up or down while I was looking?

The list re-checks every minute and whenever anyone logs a walk or playgroup. W2 urgency depends on the clock (hours since W1) and on the no-activity count, so it rises on its own. Another walker logging a walk anywhere on the roster can also shift the high-urgency and no-activity counts and raise the urgency of checkboxes on dogs nobody touched.

The order on my phone doesn't match this guide.

Right after an app update, a phone that already had the app open can keep running the old version. Fully close and reopen the app. If it still doesn't match, note the dog and the time and pass it along.
