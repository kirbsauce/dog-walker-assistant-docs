Walk Order Field Guide

WALK ORDER is the roster sort that decides which dog sits at the top of your list, and the next one, all the way down. It's built around your color: dogs you can walk come first, and dogs above your color sink to the bottom, dimmed.

How a dog finds its spot

Three questions, in order

For every dog, WALK ORDER asks the same three questions. A later question only matters when two dogs give the same answer to every earlier one.

1

Which group?

Can you walk the dog? How much activity has it had today? How urgent are its checkboxes: high, medium or low? Every dog in a higher group sits above every dog in a lower one.

2

Which color?

Inside a group, hardest dog color first. The toughest dog you can walk leads, so it gets walked while experienced walkers are on shift.

3

Which tiebreaker?

Among dogs in the same group and color: first the dog's state, then potty, priority, how long since its last activity, and yesterday's activity.

Checkbox colors

Every roster row has three checkboxes: W1, W2 and PG (labelled 1, 2 and P on the roster). Their colors drive everything below.

  • YellowHigh urgency.
  • AmberMedium urgency.
  • No colorLow urgency.
  • CyanW1 only. Reserved for training.
  • LimePG only. Reserved for playgroup.
  • Green, checkedPG only. Went to playgroup.
  • Grey, checkedWalk logged.

Which tiebreakers actually matter where

Some tiebreakers can't come up in some groups, because the same thing that would trigger them also makes W1 high urgency.

  • High-urgency groups (G1, G9): every tiebreaker is live. These are exactly the dogs that are bold potty, had no activity yesterday, or are priority 1, so the tiebreakers sort them by which reason applies.
  • Medium-urgency groups (G2, G10): the top four tiebreakers are effectively never in play, since a dog that matched any of them would be high urgency. In practice these dogs order by regular potty → priority number → quieter yesterday, then the shuffle.
  • Low-urgency groups (G3, G11): reserved and post-surgery dogs. The state order usually decides it before the tiebreakers are needed.

Example: a Gold walker's medium-urgency group (G2), mid-morning. Six dogs, no activity yet, all W1 medium urgency.

Mango and Biscuit lead because Gold is the hardest color here. Between them, Mango is a regular potty dog and Biscuit isn't.

Pepper, Tank and Ranger are all Blue. None is a potty dog, and Pepper and Tank have no priority set, which puts them above Ranger's −1. Between Pepper and Tank, Pepper had the quieter yesterday: 1 walk against Tank's playgroup.

Juno (Aqua) comes last. It's the easiest color in this group, so no tiebreaker can lift it above the Blue dogs.

Common questions

Why did a dog jump up or down while I was looking?

The list re-checks every minute and whenever anyone logs a walk or playgroup. W2 colors depend on the clock (hours since W1) and on how many dogs are still waiting, so they turn on by themselves. Another walker logging a walk anywhere on the roster can also shift the "still waiting" counts and raise the urgency of checkboxes on dogs nobody touched.

Why does my list look different from another walker's?

The list is built around your color. A Blue walker's top dog may be a Gold walker's sixth, because the Gold walker's list starts with Gold dogs. Dogs above your color also differ: what's dimmed for one walker is fully walkable for another.

Why isn't the same dog first every morning?

The daily shuffle. When dogs are genuinely equal on everything (same group, color, state, potty, priority and yesterday) they'd otherwise sit in sheet order, so the same dog would lead every day. The shuffle re-deals them at midnight. Every phone sees the same deal on the same day, so two walkers comparing lists will agree.

A dog with a high-urgency W2 is sitting low. Why?

Check its PG checkbox. A dog with W1 checked and a lime playgroup reservation (S10, S11) stays in G6 even when its W2 is high or medium urgency, because activity is already lined up for it. If the reservation falls through, remove it and the dog moves up to G4 or G5.

A deprioritized dog suddenly jumped near the top. Is that right?

Yes. Priority below zero normally pushes a dog down, but a dog with no activity yesterday still turns high urgency, and among dogs with no activity yet it ranks right after priority 1. Low priority is meant to lower a dog in the everyday order, not to leave it in its kennel two days running.

A surgery dog is always low urgency.

Expected. A dog with "surgery yesterday" never gets a high- or medium-urgency W1. It sits in G3, and its quiet-yesterday score puts it at the end of any tie. Its dog page shows the "Recent surgery - NO RUNNING" flag.

Why is a Blue- dog above a Blue dog?

You're on WALK ORDER 2, which bundles Blue- with Blue (and Gold- with Gold) for dogs with no activity yet. See bundling. WALK ORDER always puts Blue above Blue-.

The order on my phone doesn't match this guide.

First check the sort menu says WALK ORDER, not WALK ORDER 2. Right after an app update, a phone that already had the app open can keep running the old version. Fully close and reopen the app. If it still doesn't match, note the dog and the time and pass it along.
