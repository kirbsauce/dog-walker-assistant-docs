Best Practices

Every dog in the kennel has its own mental and physical needs. One is anxious and stressed in its kennel. One wasn't walked yesterday. One needs a potty break. One is just back from surgery. Our goal is to meet those needs, dog by dog, to help them be happy and adoptable.

Reaching that goal takes three things. First and foremost, you, the walker: your time, your attention and your judgment with each dog. Second, the app, which keeps track of every dog's needs and turns them into the list in front of you. Third, communication and collaboration: each of us is responsible for every dog at or below our color, not only the ones that match it, and no walker can cover them all alone. These best practices cover all three.

Walk order

A list built from every dog's needs

WALK ORDER, the roster's default sort, is where the app's attention to each dog comes together. It reads every dog's situation (yesterday, potty breaks, priority, surgery, reservations, how long since it was last out) and turns it into one list: the dog at the top is the one that needs you most right now. It looks after two kinds of need, mental and physical, for every dog in the kennel, not just the easy ones or the favorites.

Mental needs

  • Lower fear, anxiety and stress (FAS): the biggest mental need, and critical to a dog's chance of adoption
  • Social contact, with people and other dogs. While vital for all dogs, it's time-sensitive for puppies: their critical socialization window closes at around 14 weeks, and what they meet before then shapes how they handle people, dogs and new places for life, so every calm, positive moment with them counts.
  • Mental stimulation, such as training or new sights and smells on a walk

Physical needs

  • Exercise every day. A walk is most of the exercise a kennel dog gets. Without it, energy builds up with nowhere to go.
  • Bladder. Many dogs won't relieve themselves in their kennel and hold it until they're taken out. Bold potty dogs, in bold purple on the roster, need a break most.
  • Healing from surgery or injury. These dogs still need to get out, but gently. Their red caution lines, like Recent surgery - NO RUNNING, say how much activity is safe.

Best practices

Most of what makes a walk good for a dog happens before the kennel door opens. A walker who knows the day, the team and the dog can give each dog what it actually needs, and gets more dogs out in the same hours.

General

Every dog gets out once before any dog gets out twice

Dogs still waiting on their first walk or playgroup sit above every dog that has already been out, even when a second walk is urgent. A second walk matters, but a dog that hasn't left its kennel matters more.

A dog that doesn't get a walk today could be confined to its kennel for up to 58 hours, and 38 hours at a minimum, before it's out again.

The same idea runs all the way down the list. Among dogs that have already been out, the one that's waited longest since its last outing goes first. Dogs with less activity yesterday go ahead of dogs with more. And when dogs are equal on everything, a daily shuffle changes who leads each morning, so the same dogs aren't always last.

Data accuracy: when do I log a walk?

To keep walk history and other reporting accurate, please only log a walk when:

  • You walked the dog.
  • You've confirmed the dog was walked, but the walk isn't logged.

Please refrain from logging a walk when:

  • The dog has left the shelter.
  • The dog cannot be located.

Every log tells the whole team that a dog's need has been met. A walk that's logged but didn't happen hides a dog that still needs to go out. Dogs that have left the shelter are annotated in a different manner to maintain accurate walk history.

Before your shift

Look at the Shift Calendar

Resources → Volunteer → Shift Calendar shows who's coming today and where coverage might be thin. If you'll be one of the few walkers at the harder colors, you already have a good idea as to how your shift will go.

Read the roster before you pick up a leash

The NOT WALKED count, how many yellow boxes there are, and the playgroup and training counts tell you how the day is going. Sixty dogs not walked at noon is a different shift from thirty, and can significantly change the rate at which you need to walk dogs.

See who's here

Walker Activity lists everyone who's logged something today, with their color. Not everyone signs up for a shift, so it fills in walkers the Shift Calendar misses. Together, the two give you a good idea of who your collaborators are today.

Choosing who's next

Your color is a ceiling, not a lane. A Gold walker can walk every Pink, Green, Aqua, Blue and Gold dog. That makes those dogs a Gold walker's responsibility too. The kennel only gets walked when walkers of every color work together and keep track of each other.

Walk down, not only across

WALK ORDER puts the hardest dogs you can walk first, because fewer walkers can take them. Lower colors are still yours. When the dogs at your color are covered, keep going down the list instead of waiting for someone at that color to arrive.

Don't leave lower colors for later shifts

Easier dogs feel like someone else will get to them. On a short-staffed day, no one does. Watch the NOT WALKED count under the roster; it counts every dog, not just yours.

Know who else is here

Walker Activity shows everyone who's logged something today, with their color. If you're the only walker above Blue, the harder dogs are yours to cover first. If there are three Gold walkers, split the work so the easier dogs don't wait.

Communicate and collaborate

A shift where walkers coordinate gets more dogs out in the same hours, with the harder dogs left for the walkers who can take them and every color covered.

Before each dog

Log the walk right away

Go ahead and log the walk as soon as you've picked the dog. If for some reason you don't end up walking it, you can undo the log.

Read the whole dog page

Every time, even for a dog you've walked a hundred times. Cautions and notes change between shifts, and the dog's page is the only place that shows them all.

Start with the red caution lines. A caution like DROP DIVIDERS or Recent surgery - NO RUNNING is written for this dog specifically, so follow it over your usual routine. LIMITED ACTIVITY - SEE NOTES means the details are in the notes.

Then read the general and playgroup notes.

Plan the walk for this dog

A high-FAS dog may need a quiet route and a slower pace. A dog healing from surgery needs a short, gentle one. A dog that hasn't been out since yesterday may need a potty break before anything else. Decide before you open the door.

Know where you're going

Tap the kennel on the dog's page to jump to the Kennel Map, centered on its room.

The dog isn't there

Consider where else it might be

Tap Verify Kennel on the dog's page to check its kennel against Pet Compass.

Post on WhatsApp

Get help from the team by asking on WhatsApp; someone may know exactly where the dog is.

When all else fails, ask the front desk

The front desk usually has the most up-to-date information. Report what you find out back to WhatsApp.

Flag your findings

Post your findings on WhatsApp. This could be a new kennel number, or that the dog has left the shelter. Please allow the leads to massage the list based on your findings.

Undo your log

If you ultimately did not walk the dog, unlog the walk.

After the walk

Submit a note when something's new

A new trigger, a dog that's suddenly easier, something that helped it settle. Use Submit a Note in the Tools section of the dog's page; it comes filled in with the dog's details. Notes are how one walker's experience reaches every walker who comes after.

Report anything physical

Limping, vomiting, diarrhea, a wound, not eating: share it on WhatsApp, with a photo if possible, or with an experienced volunteer before submitting a medical ticket. You may be the only person who sees that dog up close today.

Report kennel problems

A broken latch, no water, a mess that needs cleaning: use Submit a Kennel Ticket from the dog's page.

Check the list again before the next dog

WALK ORDER keeps updating while you're out, as time passes and other walkers log. The dog at the top now may not be the one that was there when you last looked.

Common questions

Can I walk a dog further down the list first?

You can, and sometimes there's a good reason: the dog is in the room you're already in, or a volunteer lead asked. But each dog you skip above it stays in its kennel longer. When there's no reason, start at the top.

I'm Gold. Shouldn't the Green dogs go to Green walkers?

Only if Green walkers are here and have time. A Green dog needs its walk whether or not a Green walker shows up today. Your list puts the Gold dogs first because you're one of the few who can take them; once they're covered, the dogs below are yours too.
